# BuildRight Construction - Incident Reporting Procedure

Document IR-04, revision 2

## 1. Purpose

This procedure explains what every worker, supervisor and manager must do when an incident occurs on a BuildRight site. Reporting every incident, including near misses, allows us to understand causes and prevent the next accident. Nobody will be blamed for reporting an incident in good faith.

## 2. Definitions

- **Accident**: an unplanned event that causes injury, ill health or damage.
- **Near miss**: an unplanned event that did not cause injury or damage but could have (for example, a tool falling from a scaffold and landing next to a worker).
- **Dangerous occurrence**: a serious event listed by the regulations, such as the collapse of a scaffold, the overturning of a crane or a MEWP, or the failure of lifting equipment, even if nobody is hurt.
- **Lost-time injury (LTI)**: an injury that prevents the worker from doing his or her normal job for at least one full shift after the day of the incident.

## 3. Immediate actions (first minutes)

1. **Make the area safe**: stop the work, switch off equipment, and prevent other people from being exposed to the danger. Do not put yourself at risk.
2. **Give first aid and call for help**: call a site first aider. For any serious injury, call the emergency services (112) immediately, then inform the site office so that someone can guide the ambulance to the location.
3. **Inform your supervisor** immediately, by radio or phone.
4. **Preserve the scene**: do not move equipment, materials or debris unless needed to rescue someone or to prevent further danger. Take photos if it is safe to do so.

## 4. Within the first 24 hours

The following actions must be completed within 24 hours of any incident (accident, near miss or dangerous occurrence):

- **The worker involved** (or a witness if the worker is unable to) must report the incident verbally to the supervisor straight away and then **fill in the Incident Report Form IR-F1** (paper form in the site office or the mobile app "SafeSite Report") **within 24 hours**. The form describes what happened, where, when, who was involved, the injuries and the immediate actions taken.
- **The injured worker must see a doctor or occupational health service** within 24 hours, even if the injury seems minor, and give the medical certificate to the site office.
- **Witnesses** must write a short statement on the witness form IR-F2 on the same day, while their memory is fresh.
- **The supervisor** checks that the form is complete and sends it to the Site Safety Officer (SSO) and the Site Manager before the end of the shift.
- **The Site Manager** informs the HSE Director by phone within 1 hour for any serious accident (hospitalisation, fracture, amputation, loss of consciousness) or dangerous occurrence, and by e-mail within 24 hours for all other accidents.
- **The HSE Director** notifies the labour inspectorate and the insurer within the legal time limits. In case of a fatality, the authorities are notified immediately and in any case within 8 hours.

## 5. Investigation

- Near misses and minor injuries: investigated by the supervisor and the SSO within 3 working days.
- Lost-time injuries and dangerous occurrences: formal investigation led by the Site Manager with the SSO and a worker representative, started within 48 hours and closed within 10 working days.
- The investigation uses the "5 Whys" method to identify root causes, not only immediate causes.
- Each investigation produces corrective actions with an owner and a due date, recorded in the action tracker.

## 6. Communication and follow-up

- A safety alert summarising the incident and the lessons learned is shared at the next toolbox talk on all BuildRight sites.
- The SSO tracks corrective actions every week until they are closed.
- Monthly safety statistics (number of incidents, near misses, LTI frequency rate) are displayed on the site notice board.

## 7. Return to work

A worker returning after a lost-time injury meets the supervisor and, if needed, the occupational health doctor before resuming work. Adapted tasks may be proposed during recovery.
